If you've been getting wildly different quotes for a software project, you're not alone. We regularly talk to business owners who've received proposals ranging from ₹1 lakh to ₹15 lakh for what sounds like the same requirement. That gap isn't dishonesty — it's the reality of how software complexity works. This guide breaks down what actually drives cost, what you should expect to pay at different project sizes, and what to watch for when evaluating proposals.

## The honest answer: it depends on three things

Every software project is priced based on three factors. Scope (what does it need to do), complexity (how hard is each thing to do), and who builds it (their experience, process quality, and overhead). A rough wireframe can turn into a dramatically different amount of work depending on the answers.

That said, vague answers aren't useful. Here are the ranges we actually see in the Indian market in 2026.

## Cost ranges by project type

**Simple business website or landing page: ₹25,000–₹1,20,000**

A website with 5–10 pages, no backend, no custom integrations. Static content, contact form, maybe a blog. At the bottom end you're looking at template-based work. At the top, fully custom design and build with Next.js or similar, mobile-optimised, with technical SEO setup. The range is wide because "website" means different things to different agencies.

**Custom web application (internal tool, client portal, dashboard): ₹2,50,000–₹8,00,000**

This is where most B2B software projects fall. An internal operations management tool, a customer self-service portal, a commission calculation engine, a booking and scheduling system. The main cost drivers here are authentication complexity, number of user roles, and how much existing data or systems it needs to integrate with.

**E-commerce platform (custom): ₹1,50,000–₹6,00,000**

Shopify or WooCommerce at the low end — you're mostly paying for setup, theme customisation, and configuration. A fully custom e-commerce backend with your own inventory logic, custom payment flows, or B2B pricing rules will cost significantly more. Indian-specific requirements (UPI, COD, GST-compliant billing) add scope, though any competent team should handle these without charging extra.

**Mobile app (iOS + Android): ₹4,00,000–₹15,00,000**

React Native cross-platform at the lower end, native iOS/Android at the higher end. The range is driven by backend complexity more than the app itself — an app that shows data from a simple database is very different from one with real-time features, offline sync, payment processing, or complex user-generated content.

**Enterprise SaaS or multi-tenant platform: ₹10,00,000–₹40,00,000+**

Multi-tenant architecture, subscription billing infrastructure, admin dashboards, API layer for integrations, compliance requirements — this is a serious software product. Expect to spend at least 6–12 months and budget accordingly. This shouldn't be someone's side project.

## What makes projects more expensive

**Integrations.** Every third-party API you connect to (payment gateway, WhatsApp, ERP, accounting software) adds scope and testing time. The integration itself might be a week. Handling error states, retries, and keeping it working when the third party changes their API — that's where the real time goes.

**Real-time features.** Live dashboards, chat, notifications, collaborative editing — all of these require WebSocket infrastructure or polling mechanisms that are significantly more complex than standard request-response APIs.

**Custom reporting.** Businesses almost always underestimate how much work dashboards and reports take. A simple bar chart from a database query is trivial. A parameterised report with drill-down, date filtering, export to Excel, and grouping by multiple dimensions is not.

**Indian compliance requirements.** GST integration, Aadhaar/PAN verification, NBFC regulatory compliance, data localisation requirements — these add real scope. Don't let an agency tell you these are quick to implement; they require ongoing maintenance as regulations change.

**Mobile-first for India.** Low-bandwidth conditions, older Android devices, and high-volume SMS/OTP flows are all things a good Indian software team will design for. Teams that don't have this experience will ship something that breaks for half your users.

## What makes projects cheaper (not always a good sign)

**Offshore-to-offshore sub-contracting.** Some agencies in Indian cities quote low because they're sub-contracting the actual work to even cheaper developers, adding their own margin. Quality control is a real risk here.

**No discovery phase.** Any agency that sends you a quote without asking detailed questions about your workflows, your existing systems, your users, and your technical requirements is either guessing or quoting for something generic that won't match what you actually need.

**Hourly rates that seem too good.** ₹200–₹300/hour for senior development work doesn't exist at quality levels that will produce maintainable code. Junior developers billing at those rates will cost you more in debugging, rewrites, and support than you saved on the initial build.

## How to evaluate a proposal

First, check if the scope is specific. A good proposal describes what the system will do in enough detail that both parties have the same picture. Vague scope means vague accountability when something is missing.

Second, ask what's not included. Maintenance, hosting, bug fixes post-launch, content population, third-party account setups, analytics configuration — these are commonly excluded and then charged as add-ons.

Third, ask about the technology stack and why. Modern, maintainable choices (Next.js, TypeScript, PostgreSQL, Vercel/AWS) indicate a team that thinks about long-term health. Unusual or obscure stacks are a red flag unless there's a very good reason.

Finally, ask for references from projects of similar scope. Code is easy to show screenshots of. Speaking to a previous client about delivery process, communication quality, and what happened when problems came up — that's where you learn something real.
