Small business owners in India ask us about chatbots constantly. Usually triggered by seeing a competitor's website with a chat widget, or reading about AI, or attending a seminar where someone demonstrated ChatGPT. The conversation almost always starts with "can I get a chatbot like that?" and the honest answer is: probably yes, but let's figure out what you actually need.

## The chatbot landscape in 2026

The term "chatbot" covers an enormous range of things. At one end: a simple rule-based decision tree that helps visitors navigate your website. At the other: a full AI assistant with access to your business data, capable of understanding nuanced queries and taking actions. The cost, complexity, and usefulness of these are completely different.

For most small businesses, the right answer is somewhere in the middle. Let's break down what actually makes sense.

## What small businesses are actually trying to solve

When we dig into why a business owner wants a chatbot, it usually comes down to one of three things:

**Answering repetitive enquiries without staff.** "What are your timings?" "What's the price of X?" "Do you deliver to Y?" These questions come from prospects who won't call or email, and having someone manually answer them is expensive. A chatbot that handles these 24/7 is genuinely valuable.

**Capturing leads outside business hours.** Many small businesses miss enquiries that come in after 6pm or on weekends. A chatbot that can collect name, phone number, and requirement — and notify the owner by WhatsApp — solves a real revenue problem.

**Filtering and routing inbound queries.** For businesses that get high volumes of inbound messages (service businesses, e-commerce, etc.), having an AI triage layer that separates "I want to place an order" from "I have a complaint" from "I'm a sales rep trying to sell something" reduces the mental load on staff.

## What chatbots can't do well

Answer questions that require judgment, context, or relationship. A long-term customer asking for an exception to a policy needs a human. Someone with a complex technical question about your product likely needs a human. Chatbots work best on high-volume, low-complexity interactions.

Handle conversations that go off-script in emotional directions. A customer who's upset about a delivery issue needs empathy. Most AI systems handle this clumsily.

Work without clean data. If your product catalogue, pricing, or FAQs aren't in a structured, maintained format, the chatbot can't answer questions from them. Garbage in, garbage out.

## Types of chatbots and what they cost

**Rule-based FAQ chatbot (website widget):** Intercom, Freshchat, Tidio, or similar. Mainly drag-and-drop flow builder with pre-defined responses. These are tools, not custom development — setup takes a few days, costs ₹1,500–₹5,000/month in platform fees, and one-time setup cost of ₹15,000–₹40,000 if you want someone to do it for you.

**AI-powered website chatbot (with LLM behind it):** A custom-trained AI assistant connected to your business data (product catalogue, FAQs, service descriptions). When someone asks a question, the AI searches your data and generates a relevant answer. More capable than rule-based, but requires building and maintaining the data source it answers from. Setup: ₹50,000–₹1,50,000. Monthly: LLM API costs (typically ₹3,000–₹15,000 depending on volume) plus hosting.

**WhatsApp chatbot:** The same AI capabilities, but delivered through WhatsApp instead of a website widget. This is often more appropriate for Indian businesses whose customers are more comfortable on WhatsApp than navigating a website. Requires WhatsApp Business API access (see our [WhatsApp automation guide](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-automation-wholesale-mandi)). Setup: ₹60,000–₹2,00,000.

**Voice chatbot / IVR replacement:** AI that answers phone calls. Technically feasible but significantly more complex. Wait until your business genuinely needs it.

## How to evaluate AI chatbot vendors

Ask them to show you a live chatbot they've built for a similar business, and actually try to break it. Ask it edge-case questions, questions it wasn't trained on, questions in Hinglish. See how it handles uncertainty (does it make things up, or does it say "I don't know, here's how to reach a human"?).

Ask how they handle hallucinations. LLM-based chatbots can confidently state incorrect information if they're not properly constrained to your data. A good implementation uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — the AI searches a verified knowledge base before answering, rather than generating from model knowledge. Ask if they use this approach.

Ask what happens when the chatbot can't answer. Every good chatbot needs a graceful handoff to a human. What does that look like — a phone number, a WhatsApp link, a form? This matters more than the AI capability in most cases.

## What we recommend

Start simpler than you think you need. A rule-based FAQ bot that covers your 20 most common questions, plus a lead capture flow, will serve most small businesses well. Once you're seeing what questions come in that the bot can't handle, you'll have real data to inform whether and how to upgrade.

Don't build a chatbot for the sake of having one. If your business gets 3 enquiries per week, the chatbot will have nothing to do. The ROI on automation only materialises at volume.
