We use all three of these platforms on client projects. Not because we're indecisive, but because they genuinely serve different use cases. This comparison is based on actual implementation experience, not feature-list marketing.

## Quick context on what these tools do

Zapier, Make (formerly Integromat), and n8n are all automation platforms that let you connect software tools and trigger automated actions. The classic example: when someone fills out a form on your website, automatically add them to your CRM, send a confirmation email, and notify your sales team on Slack. You build this as a visual workflow, not code.

The main differences are in pricing model, complexity ceiling, self-hosting options, and how they handle edge cases.

## Zapier

**Best for:** Non-technical teams, simple 2–3 step automations, businesses where the tools they're connecting all have Zapier integrations.

Zapier has the largest app library — 6,000+ integrations — and the most polished, beginner-friendly interface. If you need to connect Mailchimp to Google Sheets to Slack, Zapier can do it with minimal friction.

The problems emerge at scale. Zapier's pricing is per-task (per automation step that runs), which gets expensive fast if you're running high-volume workflows. 10,000 tasks/month costs ₹8,000–₹12,000/month depending on your plan tier. For a business automating thousands of daily transactions, this adds up.

Multi-step logic and conditional branching in Zapier is possible but clunky. Complex workflows feel like fighting the tool rather than working with it.

Error handling is basic. When a step fails, you get an email. You don't get sophisticated retry logic, dead-letter queues, or structured error reporting without significant workarounds.

**Verdict for Indian SMEs:** Good starting point if your automation needs are simple and you're not technical. Outgrow it fast if you need complexity or volume.

## Make (formerly Integromat)

**Best for:** Medium-complexity workflows, teams that want more control than Zapier without going fully technical, scenarios with branching logic and data transformation.

Make's visual canvas is genuinely better than Zapier's for complex workflows. You can see the full flow at once, handle multiple branches, and transform data between steps using a function library. The pricing is operation-based too, but significantly cheaper than Zapier for equivalent workloads.

Make's 1,000 operations/month free tier is generous, and paid plans start from around ₹800/month — much more accessible than Zapier for Indian businesses.

The learning curve is steeper than Zapier. Make's interface makes sense once you get it, but the initial setup for complex scenarios takes longer. The documentation is decent but assumes some logical thinking about data flows.

One significant limitation: Make's app library is smaller than Zapier's, and some Indian-specific integrations (Tally, some Indian payment gateways, WhatsApp BSP APIs) require custom HTTP modules rather than native integrations.

**Verdict for Indian SMEs:** Our recommendation for most medium-complexity automation needs where cost matters. Better capability-to-price ratio than Zapier for almost every use case we've encountered.

## n8n

**Best for:** Technically confident teams, high-volume workflows where per-operation pricing would be expensive, scenarios requiring custom logic, businesses that want to self-host for data privacy.

n8n is open-source and can be self-hosted. This has major implications. On self-hosted n8n, you pay server costs (₹1,500–₹5,000/month on a VPS) rather than per-operation fees. For a business running 100,000+ automations per month, the savings are enormous.

The workflow builder is excellent — arguably better than Make for complex scenarios. You can write JavaScript inside workflow nodes for custom logic, which eliminates the ceiling that visual-only tools hit eventually.

The trade-off is operational overhead. Self-hosting means you manage the server, handle updates, monitor uptime, and fix issues when the platform has problems. n8n Cloud (managed) removes this but brings per-execution pricing back.

n8n also has a strong and growing app library including Zoho integrations, WhatsApp Business API, and many Indian payment gateways.

**Verdict for Indian SMEs:** If your team has someone technical who can manage a VPS, and your automation volume is high or growing, n8n is the most cost-effective choice at scale. Not recommended if you need a non-technical team to maintain the workflows independently.

## How we actually choose

For a new client with straightforward automation needs (a few workflows, moderate volume, non-technical team): Make.

For a client with high-volume workflows or a developer on their team: n8n, self-hosted.

For a client who's already on Zapier and it's working fine: we don't migrate them. Switching costs are real.

For anything requiring custom code, business logic beyond what visual tools handle, or integration with systems that have no pre-built connectors: custom development in Node.js/Python, not these platforms at all.

## What they all get wrong

All three platforms struggle with the same things: robust error handling in production (what happens when step 3 of 10 fails and needs to retry with modified data?), proper logging and auditability for compliance-sensitive workflows, and performance under true high-concurrency load.

For mission-critical business processes — payroll, financial reconciliation, customer billing — we generally don't rely purely on these platforms. We build custom systems where the reliability and auditability requirements are higher than what no-code tools reliably provide.
