"Billing software" is one of the most searched software terms in India, and it's easy to see why. Every shop, restaurant and pharmacy needs it, and most owners pick one in a hurry: whatever a friend uses, or whatever came free with the printer. Six months later they're fighting slow bills, stock numbers that don't match and GST reports that need manual fixing.

We build POS and billing systems, including our own [Qiva POS](/portfolio/qiva-pos-demo). Here's what we tell restaurant and shop owners to check before they choose one.

## The features that matter

Start with billing speed. At a busy counter, a bill should take seconds. Look for barcode scanning, quick search by item name or code, saved favourites for common items and keyboard shortcuts. Ask for a demo and time it yourself.

GST invoices should be correct without manual work. The software should handle HSN or SAC codes, CGST, SGST and IGST, and print or send invoices in the right format. Larger businesses above the e-invoicing turnover limit (currently ₹5 crore) also need e-invoice generation. Check the current rules with your CA.

Stock should update with every sale. If inventory sits in a separate tool, it will drift. Good billing software deducts stock automatically, warns you when an item runs low and records purchases from suppliers.

Payments should be built in. UPI QR codes on the bill, card machines and split payments such as part cash and part UPI are normal now, and the software should record all of them.

You also need reports you'll actually read: daily sales, best sellers, slow movers, payment-mode totals and profit by item. If you can't see last week's numbers on your phone in two taps, the reports won't get used.

## Extra features for restaurants

Restaurants need a few things that retail shops don't:

- table management and running orders
- kitchen order tickets (KOT) sent to the kitchen printer or a kitchen screen
- menu modifiers such as extra cheese, half plate or less spicy
- online orders from aggregators and QR menus landing in the same system
- recipes, so selling one paneer tikka deducts the right amount of paneer from stock

QR ordering is a good example of where things are going. We built a QR menu and ordering system for [Cafe Forest Floor](/portfolio) in Udaipur: guests order from the table, the kitchen screen sounds an alert, and the owner tracks loyalty stamps and sales reports from an admin panel.

## Cloud or offline?

Cloud software lets you check sales from anywhere and keeps your data safe if the counter PC breaks. Offline software keeps working when the internet drops, which still happens in many markets. The best systems do both: they bill offline and sync when the connection comes back. Ask every vendor exactly what happens when the internet goes down in the middle of a rush.

## What it really costs

Ready-made billing software in India ranges from free basic tools to a few thousand rupees a month for multi-counter restaurant systems. Before comparing prices, add up:

- hardware: receipt printer, barcode scanner, cash drawer, maybe a tablet for the kitchen
- per-device or per-outlet fees as you grow
- setup and loading your item list, which can take days for a shop with thousands of products
- support: who you call at 9 PM on a Saturday when the printer stops

Custom billing software makes sense when your business has something unusual, like weighing-scale integration, a franchise network, wholesale and retail pricing together, or a tight link to your website and WhatsApp orders. For most single outlets, a good ready product set up properly is the cheaper path.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Choosing on price alone. A free tool that slows every bill by ten seconds costs you customers in a queue.
- Skipping the item master. Messy product names and missing GST rates cause most reporting problems. Clean them once, at the start.
- No backups. If the software is offline-only, make sure the data is backed up automatically every day.
- No staff roles. Cashiers shouldn't be able to delete bills or change prices. Check that the software has user permissions.
