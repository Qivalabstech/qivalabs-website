"Mobile app development" and its Dubai variants are among the most searched software terms in the UAE, according to Google Trends, and the market is crowded with agencies promising the same thing. An app built for the UAE does have a few needs that a generic quote often misses, though. Here is what we tell business owners in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah before they choose who builds their app.

## What makes a UAE app different

### Two languages, two directions

Most UAE apps need Arabic and English. Arabic runs right to left, so every screen needs a mirrored layout, Arabic typefaces that stay readable at small sizes, and content that's written, not just machine-translated. Adding Arabic at the end of a project usually means redoing half the screens. Plan it from the first design.

### WhatsApp is part of the product

Customers in the UAE expect to order, book and get support on WhatsApp. An app that sends confirmations and reminders through the official WhatsApp Business API, and lets customers chat with your team there, often gets more use than one that relies on push notifications alone.

### Local payments

Card payments through a UAE gateway, Apple Pay and Google Pay are standard. Many retail and food businesses still need cash on delivery. Make sure your quote covers the payment provider your bank supports.

### A Monday-to-Friday week

Since 2022 the UAE has worked Monday to Friday. That matters for things like delivery slots, booking calendars and support hours inside the app.

## What it costs

UAE agencies price in a wide band, and a lot depends on whether the team is local, offshore or a mix. Because the dirham is pegged at 3.6725 to the US dollar, it's easy to compare against global ranges:

| Type of app | Typical range (USD) | Typical range (AED) |
|---|---|---|
| Simple app with little backend | $8,000 to $25,000 | AED 29,000 to 92,000 |
| App with accounts, payments and admin panel | $20,000 to $60,000 | AED 73,000 to 220,000 |
| Marketplace, delivery or booking platform | $50,000 to $150,000 | AED 184,000 to 550,000 |

For context, published 2026 guides put a simple app at $15,000 to $40,000 ([GoodFirms, via Zoho](https://zoho.com/creator/application-development/the-cost-of-app-development.html)) and most US-built apps at $50,000 to $250,000 ([Chop Dawg](https://www.chopdawg.com/app-development-cost-in-the-united-states-in-2026/)). Arabic support adds design and testing time, usually 10 to 20 percent on top. Plan for 15 to 25 percent of the build cost each year for maintenance.

## How long it takes

Most business apps need 10 to 16 weeks for a first version: about three weeks of discovery and design, eight to ten weeks of building, two weeks of testing, then App Store and Google Play review. Bilingual apps sit at the longer end.

## Data protection in the UAE

Personal data on the UAE mainland is covered by Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data, usually called the PDPL. Companies in the DIFC and ADGM free zones follow their own GDPR-style laws instead. One quirk: the PDPL's executive regulations, which set details like breach deadlines, [had still not been published in 2026](https://nhimg.org/uae-pdpl-guide), and [Chambers](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/investing-in-2026/657/) describes the law as in force but not yet enforced.

That isn't a reason to ignore it. Build for clear consent, collect only the data you need, limit who can see it, and host in a UAE cloud region if your sector or customers expect data to stay in the country. Doing this now is far cheaper than retrofitting it once enforcement starts. Health, finance and government projects have extra sector rules, so check with your legal adviser.

## Local agency or offshore team?

A Dubai agency gives you face-to-face meetings and local market knowledge. An offshore team can cost a lot less for the same work. With India only 1.5 hours ahead of the UAE, an Indian team shares almost your entire working day, which is a real advantage over teams in Europe or the Americas.

Whichever you choose, check the same things:

1. Live apps you can install, ideally with Arabic support.
2. Who owns the code, the store accounts and the cloud account when the project ends. It should be your company.
3. Whether the quote includes the admin panel, testing on real devices and store submission.
4. How you'll see progress. Weekly test builds are the minimum.
5. What year-one support costs after launch.

## Websites first?

Many UAE businesses don't need an app on day one. A fast, bilingual website with WhatsApp ordering or booking can test demand for a fraction of the cost, and the backend can later power the app. We often suggest this route for restaurants, clinics, salons and retailers.
