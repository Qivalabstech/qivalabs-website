If you have asked three agencies to price the same app idea, you have probably seen three numbers that look like they came from different planets. That's normal. Published 2026 guides put a simple app anywhere from about $15,000 to $40,000 ([GoodFirms, via Zoho](https://zoho.com/creator/application-development/the-cost-of-app-development.html)), while [Chop Dawg](https://www.chopdawg.com/app-development-cost-in-the-united-states-in-2026/), a US agency, says most apps built by US teams land between $50,000 and $250,000.

Both can be right. They are pricing different scopes, with different teams, in different places. Here is how we explain it to founders and business owners in the US, UK, Australia and the UAE before they sign anything.

## The four things that set the price

### How many kinds of user the app has

An app where everyone sees the same screens is one product. A delivery app has customers, drivers and a restaurant or store owner, and each of them needs their own screens, permissions and flows. Every extra user type adds something close to a small app of its own.

### Whether there is a backend

Logins, saved data, payments, notifications and an admin panel all need a server side that someone has to design, build, host and secure. On most business apps the backend is about half the work, and it's the part cheap quotes tend to leave out.

### Real-time features and integrations

Live tracking, chat, bookings that sync with a calendar, or a link to Salesforce, Shopify or your accounting software each bring their own testing and edge cases. One line in a proposal can mean two weeks of work.

### The team and where it sits

This is the lever most people underestimate. [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/blog/app-development-cost-usa-state-pricing-guide) puts US mobile developers at $50 to $150 or more an hour. [DistantJob's 2026 figures](https://dev.to/empiricinfotechllp/the-real-cost-of-hiring-offshore-vs-onshore-developers-in-2026-54kk) put senior developers in Western Europe at $60 to $120, Eastern Europe at $45 to $90 and India at $18 to $45. The same 1,000-hour build can cost several times more depending on who builds it.

## The costs that come after launch

Most guides agree on one number: plan for maintenance at roughly 15 to 25 percent of the build cost each year ([Zoho](https://zoho.com/creator/application-development/the-cost-of-app-development.html)). That covers new iOS and Android versions, security updates, bug fixes, hosting and small changes. On top of that come Apple's $99 developer fee each year, Google Play's one-time $25 fee, and any paid services the app uses, such as maps, SMS or AI models.

A quote that is much lower than everyone else's often skips one of these: the admin panel, testing on real devices, store submission or the first months of support. Ask what is included before you compare numbers.

## How long it takes

A usable first version takes three to five months for most business apps:

- discovery and screen design: 3 to 4 weeks
- building the backend and the app: 8 to 14 weeks
- testing and fixes: 2 to 3 weeks
- App Store and Google Play review: from a few days to two weeks

You can go faster by cutting scope, not by adding people halfway through. Launching with the three features users can't live without, then adding the rest based on real usage, is nearly always cheaper.

## Cross-platform or native?

For most business apps we recommend Flutter or React Native. One codebase runs on iPhone and Android, which usually saves 30 to 40 percent against building two native apps, and users can't tell the difference. Native Swift and Kotlin still make sense for heavy graphics, deep hardware access or apps where every millisecond counts.

## Five questions to ask any app development company

1. Can I install two apps you built that are live in the stores today?
2. Who owns the source code, the developer accounts and the cloud account when we finish? The answer should be you.
3. Will I get working test builds every week or two, or only near the end?
4. What exactly is in the quote: design, backend, admin panel, testing, store submission, launch support?
5. What will year one cost after launch?

If a company can't answer these clearly, the low price won't stay low.
