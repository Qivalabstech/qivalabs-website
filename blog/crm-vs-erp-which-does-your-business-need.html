CRM vs ERP: which one does a small business in India actually need?

A CRM helps you win and keep customers. An ERP runs what happens after the sale. Here is how to tell which problem you have, and what each one costs.

Rajamuddin

CTO and Designated Partner, QivaLabs · Updated 23 Nov 2026

"Should we get a CRM or an ERP?" is one of the questions we hear most from business owners in Udaipur and across Rajasthan. The honest answer depends on where your business is losing time or money, and the two systems fix very different problems.

The short version

A CRM manages your customers: leads, follow-ups, deals, support requests and the history of every conversation. It helps you sell more and keep the customers you have.

An ERP manages your operations: stock, purchases, billing, accounts, production and often HR and payroll. It helps you deliver what you sold without mistakes, and it tells you whether you are making money.

If your problem is "we forget to follow up and lose deals", that's a CRM problem. If your problem is "we sold something we didn't have in stock and the invoice was wrong", that's an ERP problem.

Signs you need a CRM

  • Leads arrive on WhatsApp, Instagram, phone calls and your website, and nobody can see all of them in one place.
  • Follow-ups depend on one salesperson's memory or a notebook.
  • When a salesperson leaves, their customer history goes with them.
  • You can't say how many enquiries became paying customers last month.

Searches for "best CRM software for small business" and "customizable CRM software" have been climbing in India over the past year, according to Google Trends. Owners are realising that a shared Excel sheet stops working at around 50 active leads.

Signs you need an ERP

  • Stock figures in the system never match what is actually on the shelf.
  • Billing, inventory and accounts live in three different tools, and someone re-types data between them every day.
  • Month-end closing takes days, and you only find out your margins weeks later.
  • Branches or warehouses can't see each other's stock.

Inventory problems are usually the first trigger. Searches for "inventory management software India" are rising quickly too, and most of our ERP conversations start with stock.

What each one costs

Ready-made CRM tools in India start from a few hundred rupees per user per month. They work well if your sales process is standard.

ERP software prices vary much more. Off-the-shelf ERP subscriptions for small businesses run from a few thousand rupees a month to several lakh a year once you add users, modules and implementation. A custom ERP built around your process typically costs ₹5 lakh to ₹20 lakh as a one-time build, plus hosting and support.

Implementation is the hidden cost in both cases. Setting up the software, importing your old data and training staff often takes as long as choosing the tool did.

When custom beats off-the-shelf

Off-the-shelf software is the right choice for most small businesses. Custom makes sense when:

  • your process is unusual and is part of why customers choose you, such as made-to-order products, mandi trading or multi-step approvals
  • you need the system to talk to WhatsApp, your website and your billing software in ways ready tools don't support
  • per-user subscription fees across a large team cost more than a one-time build over three years
  • you've tried two ready tools and your team still runs half the work in Excel

A middle path also works: a ready base customised for your business. We do this often with Qiva POS for billing and inventory, adding the parts a client's process needs.

Do you need both?

As businesses grow, yes. Usually the CRM comes first, because sales problems hurt revenue straight away. The ERP follows when operations start to break. What matters is that the two systems share data, so a won deal in the CRM becomes an order, a stock movement and an invoice without anyone typing it again.

A simple way to decide

Write down the three tasks that waste the most hours in your business every week. If they're about chasing customers, start with a CRM. If they're about stock, billing or accounts, start with an ERP. If you can't tell, talk to us. We'll look at how your team works today and tell you which system to start with, even if the answer is a ready tool we don't build.
