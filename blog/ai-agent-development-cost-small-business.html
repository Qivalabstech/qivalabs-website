AI agent development cost in 2026: what small and mid-sized businesses really pay

AI agents can cost $500 or $500,000. Here is what you get at each level, what it costs to run every month, and how to test the idea before you commit.

Rajamuddin

CTO and Designated Partner, QivaLabs · Updated 23 Nov 2026

If you have searched for AI agent development costs, you have seen price ranges all over the place. One 2026 guide from Tekrevol puts development anywhere from $8,000 to over $400,000. That range is so wide it's useless on its own, so here is how it breaks down and what we tell small and mid-sized businesses before they spend anything.

First, what counts as an AI agent

A chatbot answers questions. An AI agent does things: it reads an email, checks your CRM, drafts a quote, books a meeting or updates a ticket, and it decides which step comes next. Most businesses that ask for an "agent" need something in between, such as an assistant that answers from their own documents and hands off to a person, or a workflow that uses AI for one or two steps inside a normal automation.

That distinction matters because each step up costs more to build, test and run.

The four levels and what they cost

Level 1: off-the-shelf tools, configured

For many small businesses this is enough. DesignRush notes that most small businesses can launch AI for under $5,000, or $20 to $100 per user per month, using existing tools instead of custom development. Think of a support assistant inside your help desk, or AI features already built into your CRM.

Level 2: a custom assistant on your own data

A chatbot on your website or WhatsApp that answers from your product catalogue, policies or past tickets, captures leads and hands hard questions to your team. Expect roughly $5,000 to $20,000 to build properly, including the knowledge base, testing and a simple admin screen.

Level 3: a workflow agent

An agent that takes actions across your tools, such as qualifying inbound leads and writing them into HubSpot, processing purchase orders from email into your ERP, or triaging support tickets. Tekrevol puts workflow agents at $25,000 to $80,000. Most of that money goes on integrations, edge cases and testing, not on the AI itself.

Level 4: multi-agent and regulated systems

Several agents working together, or anything touching health, finance or legal decisions, with audit trails, human approval steps and compliance reviews. These start around $100,000 and Tekrevol notes that multi-agent systems can pass $200,000.

The monthly bill people forget

Building the agent is only half the cost. Running it means paying for model usage, hosting, a vector database, monitoring and someone to review its answers. Tekrevol estimates running costs at $500 to over $20,000 a month depending on volume. Annual maintenance commonly adds another 15 to 25 percent of the build cost.

Two things keep that bill under control: using the smallest model that does the job well, and logging every conversation so you can see where tokens are wasted.

Where the budget really goes

From the projects we've built, the AI model is rarely the hard part. The time goes on:

  • connecting to your systems, which means your CRM, inbox, calendar, ERP or WhatsApp
  • cleaning the documents and data the agent learns from
  • deciding the rules: what the agent can do alone, and what needs a human
  • testing it against real, messy customer messages, in every language your customers use
  • building the screen your team uses to review and correct it

A vendor who quotes only for "the AI" has left out most of the project.

Start with a pilot

The safest way in is a short, paid pilot. Master of Code recommends a 30-day pilot to validate value before the full build, and we agree. Pick one process with a clear number attached, such as replies sent, leads qualified or hours saved, and run the agent on real traffic with a person checking its work.

At the end you'll know three things: whether it works, what it costs to run, and what a full version would need. If the answer is no, you've spent a fraction of a full build to find out.

Questions to ask before you hire an AI development company

  1. Which of our tools will the agent connect to, and who builds those connections?
  2. What happens when the agent isn't sure? Show me the hand-off to a person.
  3. Which model will you use, and can we switch later without rebuilding?
  4. Where is our data stored, and is it used to train anyone's model?
  5. What will it cost per month at our volume?

Our take

Most small businesses should start at level 1 or 2, prove the value on one process, and only then build a workflow agent. That's the order we follow with clients in the US, UK, Australia, Europe and the UAE, and it's why our AI projects start with a pilot rather than a six-month contract.
