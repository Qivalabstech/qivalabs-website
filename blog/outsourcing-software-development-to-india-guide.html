Outsourcing software development to India in 2026: a straight guide for US, UK and Australian companies

India can cut your development cost by half or more, or it can cost you a year. Here is what the rates really are, where projects go wrong, and how to pick a team that delivers.

Rajamuddin

CTO and Designated Partner, QivaLabs · Updated 23 Nov 2026

We are an Indian software company, so take this with the obvious pinch of salt. Still, we'd rather you outsource with your eyes open than pick the cheapest team on a freelancing site and lose six months. This guide covers what it costs, what goes wrong, and how to check a team before you pay them.

What it really costs

Rate surveys for 2026 tell a consistent story. DistantJob's figures put senior developers at:

Region Typical senior rate (USD per hour)
United States and Canada $75 to $150+
Western Europe and the UK $60 to $120
Eastern Europe $45 to $90
Latin America $45 to $85
India $18 to $45

The same source puts the fully loaded cost of a senior US developer, with salary, benefits, taxes and overheads, at around $285,000 a year. That gap is why so many startups and small businesses outsource.

The hourly rate isn't the whole cost, though. As Codersera points out, a $30-an-hour developer who needs three rounds of rework can end up costing more than a $120-an-hour one who gets it right the first time. What you are really buying is finished, working software. Compare fixed quotes for the same written scope, not hourly rates.

Where outsourced projects go wrong

Vague scope

Most failed projects we are asked to rescue started with a two-line brief and an hourly contract. Nobody wrote down what "done" meant, so the vendor billed hours and the client kept waiting. A written scope with screens, features and acceptance criteria fixes more problems than any contract clause.

Silence

You send feedback on Monday and hear nothing until Friday. Good offshore teams over-communicate: daily written updates, a demo link every week, and a named person who answers your messages.

The team you met isn't the team building it

Some agencies win the deal with senior people and then hand the work to juniors. Ask who will write the code and whether you can talk to them.

Nobody owns the code

If the repository, domain and hosting sit in the vendor's accounts, you're locked in. Everything should be in your name from day one.

Time zones are easier than they look

India runs on UTC+5:30 all year. In practice that means:

  • UK and Ireland: 4.5 to 5.5 hours of difference, so your morning and lunchtime are our afternoon. Acquaint Softtech also puts the overlap at four to five hours.
  • Central Europe: 3.5 to 4.5 hours, with five or more hours of shared working time.
  • Dubai: 1.5 hours, so nearly the whole day overlaps.
  • Sydney: 4.5 to 5.5 hours ahead of India, so your afternoon is our morning.
  • US Eastern: 9.5 to 10.5 hours, so your morning is our evening, and work moves forward while you sleep.

A US team that sends feedback at 5 pm often finds the changes ready the next morning. That only works if the team has a routine of written updates and short daily calls at a fixed time.

Contracts, IP and data

You need four things in writing:

  1. an NDA, signed before you share anything sensitive
  2. a clause that assigns all intellectual property, code, designs and content to your company once paid
  3. milestone payments tied to delivered, approved work rather than hours
  4. a data processing agreement if the team will touch personal data

If you are in the UK or EU, India has no adequacy decision, so access to personal data from India needs the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the UK's International Data Transfer Agreement. Australian companies covered by the Privacy Act stay responsible for overseas recipients under APP 8. The simplest fix in every case is to keep production data in your own cloud account, in your region, and give the outsourced team limited access.

How to vet a team in an afternoon

  • Open their live work. Click around real websites and install real apps. Screenshots on a portfolio page prove very little.
  • Ask for two references from your country, and call one.
  • Ask for a short paid discovery phase before a large build. One or two weeks of screen designs and a feature list will tell you how they think.
  • Check how they answer an awkward question, such as "what would you cut from this scope?" A good team will push back.
  • Look at their response time during the sales process. It rarely gets faster after you sign.

Agency or freelancers?

Freelancers can be excellent for small, well-defined tasks. For a product that needs design, backend, mobile, testing and support, an agency gives you one contract, one point of contact and cover when someone is ill or leaves. A dedicated team, where an agency gives you named developers who work only on your product, sits in between and suits companies that already have a product owner in-house.

When outsourcing isn't the right call

If your product needs someone in the room every day, if you can't spare anyone to answer questions and review work, or if the scope will change every week while you find product-market fit, a local developer or a small in-house team may serve you better. Outsourcing works best when you can describe what you want and someone on your side has an hour a day for it.
